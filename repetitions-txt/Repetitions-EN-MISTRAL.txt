As unrest spread, demonstrations erupted in major cities like Tehran, Qeshm, Zanjan, and Hamadan, with protesters targeting symbolic and economic hubs. The government responded by detaining at least 30 people in Tehran for “disturbing public order,” though the actual number of arrests was likely higher. Authorities also closed schools and offices in 26 of Iran’s 31 provinces for four days, a move seen as an attempt to suppress open unrest and restore a fragile calm. The protests’ geographic spread and the involvement of diverse social groups—from bazaar merchants to students—further strained public order, leading to violent crackdowns and mass arrests.

In urban centers like Tehran, demonstrators blocked major roads and ports, particularly in areas such as Bandar Abbas, where truck drivers led actions to protest low wages and rising costs. These blockades not only paralyzed transportation and commerce but also symbolized broader resistance against government policies.
Protests spread rapidly, with merchants in Tehran’s central bazaar closing shops en masse, and crowds gathering outside key locations like the Alaeddin Shopping Centre, where law enforcement used tear gas to disperse demonstrators. The scale of the protests prompted authorities to close schools and offices in 26 of Iran’s 31 provinces for four days, an attempt to curb open unrest and restore a fragile calm. The government also reported detaining at least 30 people in Tehran for “disturbing public order,” though the actual number of arrests was likely higher.
The protests were marked by defiance of state authority, with some participants chanting slogans linking economic grievances to broader political demands, such as “Neither Gaza nor Lebanon, My Life for Iran.” This wave of unrest, while largely leaderless, demonstrated a high degree of spontaneity and geographic spread, disrupting daily life and challenging the regime’s control over public spaces.

The Council extended the mandate of its Fact-Finding Mission to investigate these abuses, which included the unlawful use of force, firearms, and widespread arbitrary detentions.  The pattern of repression was reminiscent of previous uprisings, such as the 2022 Woman Life Freedom movement, but reached unprecedented levels in its scale and coordination, with evidence suggesting a systematic policy of repression by the state.
